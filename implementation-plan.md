## Multiplayer structure

- One authoritative room service validates actions and owns deck order, hands, identities, effects, timers and results. Browser rendering is presentation only.
- Send each player a public view plus their authorized private view. Spectators receive public state. Do not ship hidden opponent cards or reserve asset IDs merely to conceal them visually.
- Give each submitted action an identifier and expected state revision so retries, double-clicks and reconnects cannot draw or play twice.
- Persist committed room state and recovery credentials independently of connected browser sessions. A client refresh or room-owner change must not reshuffle, reroll or expose information.
- Use authoritative deadlines. Clients display countdowns, but neither background-tab throttling nor device clock changes may decide a timeout.
- Separate gameplay events from animation completion. Blocking animation pauses need a bounded shared duration so a stalled client cannot hold the whole game indefinitely. Detailed timing remains an implementation design item for review.
- Room ownership only controls administration. It never grants the owner access to secret hands or authority to invent game results.

## Core acceptance scenarios

1. **Setup privacy:** every player sees only their own private candidates/role and authorized public information; concealed reserve is not identifiable in models, requests, tooltips or log entries.
2. **Drawing:** each click reveals exactly one entitled card; retry/double-click cannot exceed the current draw allowance. Required draw steps do not disappear on timeout.
3. **Playing:** an invalid drop returns the card without cost. A valid drop commits if choices are complete; otherwise choices appear before commitment. No takeback after committed play under the accepted default policy.
4. **Bluffing:** every rules-eligible responder receives Respond/Pass whether or not their hand contains a valid response. The public UI cannot distinguish those cases. Optional timeout passes; mandatory choices remain pending.
5. **Turn ending:** confirmation cannot skip required discard, ending triggers, level choices or resulting responses. Apply verified ordering rather than generic card-game conventions.
6. **Reconnect:** refresh during a draw or response restores the same private seat and committed state. Private recovery code works on another device. Nickname alone does not grant seat access.
7. **Owner departure:** room management transfers without moving seats or revealing information. A disconnected player's necessary decision pauses and waits.
8. **Closure:** End Game follows accepted agreement and returns to lobby without declaring a winner. End Room closes entry/recovery. Seven-hour inactivity cleanup is distinct from a maximum match duration.
9. **End disclosure:** only after a completed match reveal all identities, remaining hands and reserve Pokémon, then offer rematch/lobby. A pending abandonment vote must not trigger disclosure.
10. **Presentation parity:** different languages and graphics settings produce identical rules and public information. Reduced motion does not remove required choices or timing cues.
